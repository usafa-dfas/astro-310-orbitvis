Make the connection.

The orbit lives in inertial space. The ground track shows the point directly beneath the spacecraft on a rotating Earth.

Six elements. One idea at a time.

Select an element to see its geometry. Drag its slider or enter a value; playback pauses so you can isolate the effect. Changing ν relocates the spacecraft. Scrubbing time moves both Earth and spacecraft.

Which element moves perigee without changing the orbital plane?

Try circular, polar, retrograde, and geosynchronous presets. Turn off Earth rotation to separate orbital motion from ground-track drift.

Read the reference geometry

I points toward the vernal equinox; K points north. Ω runs from +I to the ascending node. ω runs from that node to perigee. ν runs from perigee to the spacecraft. Orbital-plane angles follow the direction of motion.

When an angle disappears

A circle has no unique perigee: use argument of latitude u. An equatorial orbit has no unique ascending node: use longitude of perigee Π. For a circular equatorial orbit, use true longitude ℓ.

The model

Ideal two-body motion around a spherical Earth. No drag, precession, maneuvers, or third-body gravity. Earth radius: 6,378.137 km. μ: 398,600.5 km³/s². Sidereal rotation: 23 h 56 min 4.09 s.

The course’s 15°/hour is a rounded approximation. This app uses sidereal rotation throughout, so the worked examples’ pass spacing differs slightly. Synchronous a ≈ 42,164.17 km.

The map is an equal latitude/longitude (equirectangular) map, matching lesson 11. Past and future tracks are predictions of the current model; edits regenerate them. Spacecraft, vector lengths, and line thicknesses are schematic. Globe and orbit share the same distance scale.
